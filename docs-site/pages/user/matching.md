# Matching

Scout can suggest a researcher whose work fits with yours. Your work and theirs may not overlap much. One of you may bring a method the other lacks, for example.

You each see a short card that does not show your name. Names and contact details are shared only when **both** of you say yes.

Matching is optional. It is off until you turn it on.

## Turn matching on

1. Open **Profile and privacy**.
2. Turn on **Suggest me to complementary researchers**. The Matches screen also offers this switch.
3. If you want, turn on **Also use topic signals from my private vault**. This switch needs matching to be on.

Scout suggests you only when your profile has enough material. It needs a finished import of your works, at least three topics and at least one research method.

**Topic signals** are short statements from the methods section of your uploaded documents. Scout checks each one and removes e-mail addresses, links and phone numbers. Scout never sends the files themselves.

**Turn matching off** at any time. Scout then stops new suggestions. Your open proposals close at the next nightly check (02:00 UTC). The other researcher is not told why. Their proposal looks the same as a decline.

A match that you **both accepted** stays visible to you both. It ends only when one of you deletes your account.

## What you see before you decide

When Scout suggests someone, the card shows:

- a **match score**, as a percentage;
- the **field** you are matched in, the other researcher's **region** (a continent) and their **career stage** (early, mid or senior). If Scout does not know the region or the career stage, the card says "not shared";
- the **shared topics**;
- up to three things **you bring** and up to three things **they bring**. These are short descriptions of methods, such as "Experience with ...". The card has no names, titles, years or links;
- a short **why** text;
- the date the proposal is **open until**. A proposal stays open for 14 days.

Choose **Accept** or **Decline**.

## After you accept

The card says **Waiting for the other researcher**. You can take your yes back with **Withdraw** until they answer.

Scout does not tell you whether they have answered. Contact details appear only if they also say yes.

If they decline, you are not told. The card keeps saying "Waiting for the other researcher" until the open-until date. Then it says **Expired**. No contact details were shared.

## After you decline

The card says **You passed on this proposal**. It leaves your list. The other researcher is not told.

Declining is final for this proposal. You can tick **Never suggest this person again** before you confirm. Then Scout never suggests either of you to the other again. Without this tick, the same pair can be suggested again after 180 days.

## When you both say yes

The card shows:

- **Contact details**: the other researcher's name, institution, e-mail address, ORCID and OpenAlex ids, region and career stage.
- **Joint ideas**: one to three ideas for working together. Each has a title, a question, what each of you brings, a first step and a possible output. Ideas can take a short time to appear. If Scout cannot make them, the contact details still show.
- **Rating**: rate the match from 1 to 5 stars. You can add reasons (for example "Methods fit" or "Too similar to me") and a comment. A new rating replaces the old one. Ratings help the team tune matching.

## Limits and rules

- You get at most **3 new proposals in 7 days**.
- A proposal is **open for 14 days**. After that it is expired.
- The same pair is not suggested again for **180 days** after the last proposal. A "never suggest" choice overrides this.
- By default, Scout does not suggest:
  - people who co-authored a paper with you in OpenAlex;
  - people at the same institution.

  The service operator sets these rules.

## What data Scout uses

**Used for matching:**

- your **published works** in OpenAlex: titles, abstracts, years, citation counts and topics. These are public records;
- your **country**, used only as a continent;
- your **career stage**, estimated from the years since your first work;
- with topic signals on, the **verified statements** from the methods section of your documents (at most 10).

**Used after both of you accept:**

- the same topic signals, from the methods and aims sections, if topic signals are still on at that time.

**Not used:**

- the **files** in your vault;
- your **e-mail address**, ORCID or institution on the card. Your contact details are shown only to the other person after both of you accept;
- your **name, institution, years or country** in the text the AI model writes for the card. The model never sees them;
- the AI does not choose who you are matched with. Scout's own rules and scores do that.

The joint ideas step gets the titles of your recent works, and the signals. It gets no names, institutions, years or countries.

If you delete your account, the other researchers can no longer see your contact details.
